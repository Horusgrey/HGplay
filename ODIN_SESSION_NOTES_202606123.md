## THE PIVOT (what changed tonight)

**FROM:** Odin as an education platform — build the site, the courses, the student→resume loop, deploy to odin.it.com. (14 versions, zero users, one site accidentally overwritten by Visual-Co.)

**TO:** Odin as a **credential** — "the combine for stampless work." Not a school, not a job board, not a recruiter. The trusted, standardized *proof of ability* that the dying college degree used to be.

### The thesis, locked
- Content is no longer scarce (LLMs killed that). The **router** (right path, right job, right order) and the **verifier** (proof this person can actually do it) are where all the value migrated.
- In an AI-saturated world where credentials are fakeable, **proof of actual ability becomes the scarcest thing in the labor market.** That's the moat. "Proof over claims" isn't a feature — it's the whole company.
- The degree is failing as a signal: too expensive, too slow, now fakeable. Something has to replace it. Whoever builds the trusted replacement owns the credentialing layer, not just an edtech product.

### The combine analogy (the core frame — Zack's, and it's the right one)
The NFL Combine doesn't teach or place anyone. It runs everyone through a **standardized, trusted, observable measurement**, and the owners agree to believe the result. The value isn't training — it's that **every buyer trusts the same measurement.** Odin = the combine. Candidates earn a portable stamp; employers screen for it. (Beats the "SAT" framing because demand is baked in — a combine is about getting drafted.)

### Two hard truths about a stamp (the strategy, not caveats)
1. **A stamp is worth what the buyers say it's worth.** Value is conferred top-down by demand. So the first move isn't "get learners" — it's **get ONE employer with hiring power to recognize the stamp.** That confers the legitimacy that makes the stamp worth earning.
2. **Trust dies the first time the stamp is wrong.** The proof must be genuinely rigorous, deliverable-based, hard to fake. The instant an Odin-stamped person can't do the job, the stamp is worthless in that niche forever. This is *why* proof-over-claims is survival, not preference.

## THE BRAIN (the actual IP — domain-agnostic engine)

Six-stage pipeline. Two branches (regulated vs. open). One optional predictive pass. Run by hand for now; software later, only after proof.

1. **INGEST** — pasted posting OR field selection → normalized role descriptor; classify **regulated** (license gates work) vs **open** (proof gates work).
2. **DECOMPOSE** — break role into skill atoms, each tagged: *foundational* (first principles, don't change), *role-specific* (applied), *tool-specific* (perishable). **Rule: every tool atom must point to the foundational atom beneath it.** ("Knows Premiere" → "retention/pacing editing.") This is what makes Odin teach understanding, not button-pushing.
3. **SEQUENCE** — order by dependency, foundational-first. Produces the "feels like college, laser-focused" path — a graph where each step earns the next.
4. **REGULATORY OVERLAY** (regulated fields only) — cross-reference state licensing; mark exam-relevant vs. supervised-hours-required vs. hand-off-to-official-body. Odin's honest role in regulated fields: get you exam-ready, then hand you off with a map. (Licensing is a *first-class input to the brain*, not an afterthought — getting it wrong is a liability.)
5. **RESOURCE + PROVE MAP** — attach best *free* resource to each atom + a deliverable that proves it. Foundational → conceptual checks; role-specific → applied deliverables. This is where "proof" gets operationalized per-skill.
6. **FIT TO TIME (the slider)** — the brain now knows the true minimum time. Slider floors there; sliding up adds *depth, not fluff.* Two modes: **Master the field** (full scope) vs **Target this job** (minimum viable for one posting). Same engine, two front doors.

**Predictive hook (white-collar gold):** for open knowledge-work roles, optionally run a "role-evolution pass" — decompose the *AI-augmented* version of the role coming in ~18 months, not just today's posting. Teach the skills the market will demand next year, before competitors realize they're skills. (Parked behind core proof for now.)

## SOLVING THE INDEED PROBLEM (Zack's sharp catch)

**Concern:** you can't wedge yourself between Indeed and an employer's postings — you don't own that pipe.
**Resolution:** you never needed to. You route *around* Indeed, not through it. Three escalating models:
1. **Talent-supplier** — run candidates through Odin's proof on your own, hand the employer a stamped shortlist. (Like a staffing agency, but candidates come with proof not resumes. Works tomorrow, zero integration.)
2. **Opt-in "skip the line"** ← *chosen near-term approach.* Employer keeps the Indeed posting, adds one line: "Want to skip the line? Prove you can do this job at [odin link]." Motivated applicants route *themselves* to Odin. No interception — pure opt-in.
3. **Full filter** — only after the employer is a believer and asks for it. Milestone five, never the lead.

**The "not a recruiter" resolution:** recruiting is a *service* (linear, your time is the product, doesn't compound). Odin is an *asset* (a credential candidates carry everywhere — compounds, scales, no placement). The stamp lives at a public URL (`odin.it.com/proof/[name]`); candidates put it on LinkedIn/Indeed/email and become your free distribution. You certify; you never place.
